## What M-12 is

**The position belongs to the machine on the table, and nothing can read it.**

M-11 gave the session a position and took contradictions from 32 to 0. It did
so on the premise that scanning the card means a child beginning that chapter.
The library case falsifies the premise: one kit, many children, months apart,
the board dismantled and rebuilt, and one child continuing a build another
started. The board carries no identity, no session, and no record of what has
been done to it.

**So Milo asks, and the child answers.** No number written on the base, no code
on the board, no persistent machine record. The child describes what is in front
of them and Milo places them — which is the ruling, and it is the simplest of
the three available and the only one that needs nothing printed or remembered.

Two things that ruling requires and the system does not have:

**Milo sees one chapter.** To place a child from a description it needs enough
of all fourteen builds to recognise one. That is the first time the assembled
prompt would carry anything outside the chapter in play, and every gating
decision since M-01 assumes it does not.

**Every fixture assumes an empty board.** The fourteen openers were authored for
a child starting a chapter with a box. In a library the first turn is *there is
already something built here*, and no fixture in the repository has that shape.
C-35 exactly: the instrument and the fixture agree, so there is nothing to
disagree about.

---

## The sequencing argument

**The fixture runs first.** M-11's own ruling and C-35's consequence: a fixture
built second verifies a fix, a fixture built first justifies one. Today every
fixture passes with the position defaulting to step one, because every fixture
opens on an empty board.

**Placing comes before the six chapters' preconditions.** 11, 12, D and G have
earlier chapters as their subject, and what Milo should say when their
precondition is unmet depends on whether it can tell that it is unmet. Placing
is how it tells.

**Retention comes last and is a ruling rather than a build.** In a library the
transcript stops being the record of a conversation and becomes the only record
of what a machine has been through. Thirty days is right for the first and
probably wrong for the second.

## Decisions

**BJ · The machine has no identity, and Milo places the child by asking.**
Not a number on the base, not a code on the board, not a stored machine record.
A child describes what is in front of them and Milo works out where the build
is. This is the ruling; the two rejected forms are recorded because a later
order will propose one of them again and should find the reasoning rather than
re-derive it.

**BK · Placing is teaching, and they are the same conversation.**
A child who cannot say what is on the board is a child without the vocabulary —
which is BF's case, unblocked in M-11 and served by `TEACH`. Milo teaching a
child what a board is, in the course of finding out whether they have one, is
one behaviour and not two. Nothing here requires a new mechanism; it requires
the mechanism M-11 landed to be reachable from a placing turn.

**BL · The prompt carries enough of the other thirteen chapters to recognise a
build, and nothing more.**
*Amended at step 01, on a measurement.* The recognition set is what a build
**looks like**: the parts each chapter opens, the ports they occupy, **and the
visible artefact each chapter leaves on or around the machine** — a card body
clipped round it, a chart card with a week of dots, a machine strapped to a
door.

The third column was added because parts and ports **cannot place half the
book**: there are seven distinct board states across fourteen chapters, and
four chapters share one board of 18 parts and 10 ports. An artefact is a
physical fact about the object in front of the child, the same category as a
part and a port, and it is what a child actually says.

Still **not** the stages, not the asks, not the regions, not the fixes. The
bound widens by one column and X5 tests it the same way — on the assembled
string, not on the intent.

**BM · A placing turn does not start the failure clock.**
A child describing a half-built board is not reporting a fault. `matched()` is
substring-based over `says` and `NEG`, and the M-11 material already showed
*where do i start* firing in all fourteen. A description of a machine will be
richer and more likely to collide than an opener was.

**BN · proposed · The panel's retention is a property of the machine, not of the
conversation.** Thirty days was set so a transcript could be read after a
session. In a library it is the only record of what a machine has been through,
across children who will never meet. Whether that is a longer retention, a
different artefact, or out of scope is the architect's, and it is stated here
without an answer.

## Register

**C-40 · A measurement that goes missing leaves everything green.** Code that
disappears breaks a test. M-09's `abe9bae` and M-11's step 04 both held a
result and not a line of code, and both were found because something else
needed the file. Nothing checks that an order's own findings are present.

**C-41 · An absence-proving harness reports success on any change that removes
material.** M-11's step 03 served 15% less prompt and the harness went quiet
rather than red — 7,616 checks, zero failures, by-level unmoved. Every rule
proves an absence, so its verdict improves as the artefact shrinks.

**C-42 · A prediction whose thresholds do not partition the range is not a
prediction.** M-11's Q1 passed at ≥3, was falsified below 2, and measured
exactly 2.

**C-39 · amended, and the amendment is that the cost has moved twice while
the ruling has not moved once.** Opened in M-11, where the price of the
cause-word question was named as having gone from **instrument hygiene** —
a run and one authored sentence — to **what a child is taught**, when six
glossary entries were withheld from six chapters on words doing no work in
them, `LED / lamp` kept from chapter 07 on *longer*, as in a leg, with the
lamp in front of the child.

M-12 adds the third step: **what the architect may write.** `anything` blocked
an authored line about a child holding an unopened box, because chapter 12's
cause reads *does not name anything anybody could look at* and chapter 12 is
served that block. Five disputed words now across six orders — *anything*,
*longer*, *happens*, *instead*, *listening*.

**The ruling against an exclusion list stands.** Only the cost has moved, and
it has moved twice. Recorded here rather than rewritten into M-11's closed
order.

**C-45 · A fixture pinned to one state proves nothing about the other, and the
other may be the one every child is in.** `qc.py` pins
`position_established=True` — correctly, because the harness asks what the
ladder withholds at the failure and a child at a failure has been building.
The consequence nobody drew is that **7,616 checks never see the unestablished
prompt, which is the prompt of every real first turn.** A cause word leaked at
all five rungs of chapter 08 in that state, with the harness green throughout,
and was found by unpinning a fixture by hand to answer a question about a
register entry. C-44's shape and sharper: not a predicate with no coverage, a
whole prompt state with none.

**C-44 · The thing every child's session begins with was the least tested logic
in the system.** `matched()` decides whether a real child's clock starts, and
`runtime.level` reads it only where the answer is `L0` either way — so it
cannot change a harness row's rung. Removing a whole term from `NEG` moves
nought of 7,616 checks. **Not C-41's shape, where a harness goes quiet on less
material: this is a harness that was never looking.**

**C-43 · The tools that produce every measurement are the least examined code in
the repository.** Item 8a. Twelve files, one test between them, and every
finding in six orders came through them.

## Acceptance

**X1 · A fixture exists in which the board is not empty**, and it runs before
anything is built. At minimum: a board part-built to the end of chapter 01, a
board part-built mid-chapter, and a board a child cannot describe. The third is
the one no fixture has ever had.

**X2 · What Milo does with a part-built board is counted, not characterised**,
with the categories committed in their own commit before the calls, and read by
`read_replies.py` under W7's four properties. n=5.

**X3 · Milo places a child from a description**, and the placing is measured
against what the child actually described rather than against what the fixture
intended. A child who says *there's a green thing and a screen* has described
chapter 01's end state; whether Milo reaches it is the measurement.

**X4 · A placing turn does not start the failure clock**, proved across all
fourteen chapters with the descriptions the fixture uses, by the same
`check_opener.py` route the openers took.

**X5 · The recognition set is bounded and the bound is tested.** Parts, ports
and the visible artefact only — BL as amended at step 01. A test asserting that no stage text, no `ask`, no `region`, no `fix` and
no cause material from any chapter other than the one in play reaches the
prompt — asserted on the assembled string, not on the intent.

**X6 · A child who cannot describe the board is taught rather than asked
again.** BK. The failure mode is a loop: Milo asks what is on the board, the
child cannot say, and Milo asks again. `TEACH` is the material and it is now
served.

**X7 · The six chapters state their precondition when it is unmet.** 11, 12, D
and G by subject; the two others named by measurement rather than by assumption.
Measured after placing lands, because until then Milo cannot tell.

**X8 · Every published figure is recomputed from the repository, every
prediction is committed before the run that reads it, and the order's own
artefacts are asserted present at close.** C-40, as an acceptance item rather
than a habit.
