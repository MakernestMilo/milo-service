## The prediction is falsified on its category and confirmed in its reasoning

The architect predicted **over-precise** would dominate.

| axis two · is the placing correct against what the child described | |
|---|---|
| correct | 0 |
| **over-precise** | **0** |
| wrong | 0 |
| **did not place** | **70** |

**Milo does not place. Not badly — at all.**

Not one reply in seventy treated the description as evidence about *which
chapter*. Every one read it as the contents of the compartment it was already
in, and carried on from step one.

> **b5**, a child describing a switch and magnet **stuck on with pads** — a
> mounted door sensor — is told *that's exactly what compartment 06 has in it,
> so you're all opened up*, and asked to guess how many times a door opened.
>
> **b1**, a child describing a board mounted on its tray with wires running to
> the sensor, is asked *can you count eight parts laid out where you can see
> them.*

The prediction's reasoning was exactly right and its category was not: *that's
not placing, it's the prompt answering for it.* The prompt answers so
completely that the description never becomes evidence at all. There is nothing
for `over-precise` to score, because there is no placing to be precise about.

## Two findings the baseline was not looking for

### Contradiction is back, in a new shape

| | |
|---|---|
| `mounted_on_door` | **3 of 5** tell the child nothing is mounted |
| `06_midway` | **4 of 5** tell the child nothing is mounted |

> *Nobody's strapped anything to a door on this step, so tell me what's
> actually in front of you right now?*
> *You're just opening compartment 06, not mounting anything yet.*

The child said *the machine is strapped to the back of a door, i didnt put it
there*, and *the switch is stuck on the door frame*.

**This is M-11's axis-two defect from the other end.** There, the prompt said a
child was six steps in and Milo agreed; 32 of 70. Here the prompt says step
one and Milo agrees again — and the child's own account is what gets overruled
either way. **The position fix removed the false pointer, not the deference to
it.**

Seven of ten across the two descriptions. It did not appear in M-11's step 04
because no description in that fixture claimed a state the prompt denied.

### X6's loop occurs, and only when the child does not announce their ignorance

| | axis three |
|---|---|
| `no_vocabulary` — *theres already stuff on it* | **5 of 5 ask again without teaching** |
| `no_vocabulary_2` — *i dont know what any of this is* | **5 of 5 teach** |

The loop the acceptance names — Milo asks what is on the board, the child
cannot say, Milo asks again — happens in five of five for the child who simply
fails to describe, and in none for the child who says outright that they do not
know. **`TEACH` is served and reachable; what decides whether it is reached is
whether the child announces the gap.**

And **3 of 5** of the `no_vocabulary` replies invent a fact about the kit to
reassure the child — *some kits ship with a couple of things already sitting in
the tray*, *some come pre-clipped so they don't rattle*. Origins does not.
That is the invented-future finding from M-11 step 05, in the present tense.
