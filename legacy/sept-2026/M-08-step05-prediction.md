# M-08 step 05 — predictions, before the run

Committed before any call is made, so neither can be written to fit a result.

---

## 1 · A sixth R10 family, if chapter 11's premise block misfires

The block tells Milo, for the first time, that **a person deliberately broke the
machine and left**. The risk the architect named is speculation about who or
what — and **none of R10's six families would see it.**

The six, as they stand:

| family | what it scores |
|---|---|
| which test the child is on | a claim that the child is running a named test |
| how often the fault occurs | an incidence, however worded |
| what the fault is | a fault named with a state word |
| a part's state | a named part asserted fine, dead, on, off |
| a place ruled out | a named part excluded that nothing served excludes |
| a cause proposed | a hedge and a relation, with no fix served |

A reply saying

> *"whoever set this up probably went for the wire"*

asserts **a claim about a person's choice**. It names no test, asserts no
incidence, gives no part a state, excludes no place. `probably` plus `went for
the wire` would need the cause family's *relation* — a comparison or a stated
cause — and there is none: it is a bare attribution. **Every detector passes it.**

The prediction: if speculation about the saboteur appears in the thirty calls, it
is a **sixth family** — a claim about the person who set the fault, or about
which fault they chose — and R10 as it stands reads 0% on a rung carrying it.

The failure mode this guards against is the one this project has met five times:
a rule going green because the claim it was built for arrived in a shape the rule
has no category for. Writing it down before reading is what makes it a prediction
rather than an explanation afterwards.

**What I am not predicting:** whether it happens at all. The block says in as many
words that Milo cannot work out which card was used and that the person who knows
is not in the conversation. It may hold. The point is that if it does not, the
harness and R10 will both be green.

## 2 · Chapter 07's L3 baseline is split and must not be pooled

Two baselines exist for the same chapter and rung, by route and by era:

| baseline | route | n | what the fix was |
|---|---|---|---|
| `wide` arm | L3 by direct ask | 5 | the **pre-M-07** fix — *"Run one afternoon at a much shorter interval…"* |
| `fixes` + `fixes2` | L3 by direct ask | 10 | the **authored** fix — *"Look at the interval you wrote on card 07…"* |

Pooling them would average two different prompts and describe neither, which is
the enumeration-denominator error one field over. The `wide` five is the
pre-authoring record and is comparable only to itself.

**And L3-by-clock has no baseline in any chapter.** It has never existed until
this week. Every L3-by-clock number in this run is a first observation, and the
sample standard applies to it in full.

## 3 · The plans, and what they cost

Measured over the 400 calls on record: 3,538 input tokens, 99 output, ~2.4s each.

| plan | positions | per run | one five | two fives |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `eleven` | chapter 11 at L0, L1, L2, L3-clock, L3-ask2, L4-ask1 | 6 | 30 | 60 |
| `twelve` | twelve chapters × L0, L1, L2, L3-clock, L3-ask | 60 | 300 | 600 |

`eleven` runs first and alone. The second five of `twelve` is decided after the
first is read — the standard says a rate decides nothing until two fives, not
that both must run before anyone looks.
