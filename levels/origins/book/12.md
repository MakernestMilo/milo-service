First run

Test it against your own sentence, out loud, one part at a time. Does it do the thing? On the right trigger? And can you tell that it worked?

If the answer to the third one is "well, sort of", the sentence wasn't finished.

If it doesn't upload, or does something odd, that isn't the chapter going wrong. That is your first real bug. Read your code out loud to me at (makernest.com/origins/12), line by line. Most bugs are found by the person reading them out.

BREAK IT ON PURPOSE · 12 MIN
The sentence was too big

It already happened, at step 02, and you probably treated it as a formality. It wasn't. The first version of every specification ever written has been too big, including ones written by grown-ups who do this for a living. Noticing that is a skill, not an embarrassment.

Now the sharper one. Hand your sentence to somebody who hasn't been in the room, and ask them one question:

"Would you know if this had worked?"

A vague sentence makes sense to the person who wrote it and to nobody else. Most people rewrite the last part at this point. That is why this page is here.

CHECK YOURSELF

Three things: a one-sentence specification, a machine that matches it, and an answer to what you left out. The third is required. A machine with no cut list means the specification was never really written.

FOR THE GROWN-UP

Be the reader in step 05. Ask "would I know if this had worked?" and mean it. A polite yes at that moment costs the maker the whole lesson. When the code gets stuck, don't fix it. Ask them to read it aloud to you.
