The one idea

A machine needs two instructions, not one.

Start is a number. Stop is a different number, and it is a second decision that belongs to you. Nobody can work it out from the first one.

If it starts at one number, when should it stop?

BREAK IT ON PURPOSE · 12 MIN
The chatter

Hold your hand near the sensor but not touching, so the reading hovers right on your number instead of going past it.

The alarm starts flapping. On, off, on, off, every couple of seconds. Your sensor only looks at the room every two seconds, so that is as fast as it can change its mind. It is loud, it is slow, and it sounds broken.

It is not. The reading is wobbling across your line. Every tiny wobble one way means start. Every wobble back means stop. The machine obeys every single one of them, perfectly.

The fix is not a better sensor. The fix is a gap. Start at one number and stop two degrees away, and a small wobble can't cross both lines. It has to make a real journey to change the machine's mind.

Set your own gap. Two degrees, five, whatever you like. Then make it chatter again on purpose, to prove you know exactly what causes it.

CHECK YOURSELF

You should be able to explain your stop number and say why one number was not enough. Listen for some version of: it was wobbling across the line. If your answer is "the sensor was broken", the chapter is not finished. Make it chatter once more and watch the number, not the buzzer.

That is act one. Your machine can feel something, decide something, do something about it, and stop on its own.

FOR THE GROWN-UP

The chatter depends on real sensor wobble, so it may take two or three tries to find the hovering distance. That hunt is part of it. Don't hold the maker's hand steady for them.
