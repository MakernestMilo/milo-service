02

The one idea

A machine only knows what it can reach.

Where you put it isn't a detail to sort out at the end. It is part of the design. It decides what the machine can find out, and every position gives something up.

What will it be able to see from there, and what will it miss?

Build

You are going to put it in two places, and the first one is the wrong one, on purpose.

01

Upload sketch 09 Stakeout. Everything you set is still saved.

02

Open compartment 09: sticky cable clips and two cable ties. A tie through the two keyholes straps the base to a rail, a pipe or a shelf bracket. The clips hold the cable and the long leads flat along a wall.

03

Put it in the easy place first: near a socket, on a shelf, wherever the cable reaches. This is what everybody does. Run it for ten minutes and write the numbers down.

04

Now put it where the problem actually is. Stand it on its legs, strap it with the ties, or take the legs off and hang it on two screws by its keyholes.

Run it for ten minutes and write those numbers down too.

05
BREAK IT ON PURPOSE · 13 MIN
The easy place

You already did it, on purpose, at step 03. It wasn't a silly thing to do. It is what almost everybody does, including grown-ups who are paid to do this.

Now find the cost of the good place, because there always is one. Standing where the machine is now, check:

Can you read the display from anywhere useful? Does the charger reach, or is there a lead across a doorway? Will the battery last? Can the buzzer be heard from where people actually sit? Has anybody objected to it being there?

Every place is a trade. There is no position that gives up nothing.

Write down what yours gave up. Then take it down and look at the surface. The kit is meant to leave no mark. If it left one, that is a real finding and it goes on the card. I would rather know.

Show it

Take the person whose problem it was and show them the machine in place, running, with the numbers on it.

Then tell them what it can't see. Saying what your own work can't do is a useful habit, and you are the first person in the room to point it out.
