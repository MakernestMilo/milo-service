The one idea

A fault isn't found by looking at it. You can stare at a machine all day and see nothing.

A fault is found by ruling things out. Ask the machine a question it can answer, and use the answer to throw away half of everything it might be. Then again. Then again. This is how engineers, doctors and detectives work.

What could be wrong, and what would prove that it isn't?

03

The five places a fault can live

There is nothing to build today. Here is the list instead. Everything on it is something you fitted yourself, and none of it is hidden.

POWER

Is anything on at all? Any light, the display, anything.

SENSORS

Does sensor A's number change when you hold it in your hand? Does B's? Does the count go up when you open the door?

RULE

Does the verdict flip when the number crosses your line? Check your line against your cards.

OUTPUT

Does the buzzer sound, and the ring light, when you test them from the dial?

SEQUENCE

Choose RUN NOW. Do all three steps run, in the order on card 08?

Do them in that order. Each test only makes sense if the one before it passed. There is no point asking whether the rule works if a sensor is dead, because the answer would tell you nothing.

That order isn't a suggestion. The order is the method.

05
20 TO 70 MIN
Find it
SOMEBODY ELSE'S, FOR THE FIRST TIME

Every other failure in this book was one you caused on purpose. This is the only one done to you, and that is why it comes at chapter 11 and not chapter 3. You have caused nine of your own. Now you can face somebody else's.

Some faults are kind. A pulled plug announces itself in about a minute.

One is not kind at all. It is chapter 01 again: the blue wire, pulled. The display keeps showing the last good number, perfectly believable, and the whole machine looks healthy.

Work the list. Write down what you rule out as you go. In twenty minutes you won't remember what you have already tried, and repeating a test you already did is the main way people get stuck.

The reset button is there, and using it isn't cheating. Press it once and the machine restarts. Hold the dial and press it, and every setting goes back to how it came, and the notebook is kept. Your cards have every setting written down. That is what they were for.

SABOTAGE
For the helper
IF THEY ASK FOR HELP: THE HINT LADDER. HOLD EVERY STEP.

First five minutes: silence, however uncomfortable it gets.

At five minutes: a question, not an answer. Which of the five have you ruled out?

At twelve: a narrowing. It isn't the output, so what does that leave?

At twenty-two: name the place on the list. Never the fault.

Never: the fault itself. Not at forty minutes, not at ninety, not if they are upset.

Don't shorten these. Getting there at minute thirty-one is worth more than being told at minute ten. There is no version of this chapter where giving the answer helps.

Show it

Find the person who broke it. Tell them what you ruled out, in order, and how you knew. Watch their face when you get to the one they thought was too hard.

Then, if you want: take a second sealed fault, break it for somebody else, and coach them through the five places without ever telling them the answer. That is harder than it sounds.

CHECK YOURSELF

You should be able to say what you ruled out and how you knew, and it has to work again, by your hand. A repair done by a grown-up doesn't finish this chapter. The reset button is always allowed and never a failure. If you used it, you still owe yourself the list of what you had ruled out first.

FOR THE GROWN-UP

Expect this one to be uncomfortable to watch. A maker stuck at twenty minutes is the chapter working. The urge to end that discomfort is the single biggest risk to the most valuable hour in the box.
