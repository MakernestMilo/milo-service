02
3 MIN
The one idea

Your machine has no opinions. It is perfectly happy at any temperature. It does not care if you are cold.

It gets an opinion when a person picks a number and says: below this, say something. That number is called a threshold. Choosing one is a judgement. It is not a fact and not a sum. Different people would choose differently, and none of them would be wrong.

What number would make you switch off the fan, or fetch a jumper?
SKETCH 02 · TYPE THIS AT STEP 01 OF THE BUILD
#include <MakerNestOrigins.h>
void setup() {
  machine.begin(2, "02|THE|TRIPWIRE");
  machine.homeShows(SHOW_A | SHOW_LINE);
  machine.useScreen(SCR_COLD_LINE);
  machine.useScreen(SCR_NORMAL_COLOUR);
  if (machine.settings.coldLine == NOT_SET)
    machine.edit(SCR_COLD_LINE);
}
void loop() {
  machine.update();
  if (machine.temperatureA() < machine.settings.coldLine)
    machine.ring(RED);
  else
    machine.ringNormal();
}

The line with < in it is your threshold: the whole opinion of your machine.

04
7 MIN
First run

The display shows your reading, and under it WATCH BELOW with nothing after it yet.

Turn the dial. The number moves one degree each click, so you can feel every one. Stop at the number you chose and press the dial to confirm.

The ring lights up. Turn the dial to choose its normal colour: the colour that means everything is fine. Press to confirm.

Hold the sensor in your fist. The number climbs and the ring stays normal. Now hold it against a glass of cold water. The number falls, crosses your line, and the ring changes.

Walk to the far side of the room and get somebody else to do it. You can see the answer from over there. In chapter 01 you had to be close enough to read a number. That is new.

05
BREAK IT ON PURPOSE · 13 MIN
The false alarm

Turn the dial and set your number to 40 degrees.

The room is nowhere near 40. The ring goes to its alarm colour straight away and stays there. Your machine now insists this room is too cold, and it will insist all evening.

The machine is not broken and it is not wrong. It is doing exactly what it was told. The judgement was bad, and the judgement was yours.

Now set it to 5 degrees. Hold the sensor against the coldest thing you can find. Nothing. This room could freeze and your machine would never say a word.

Same mistake, two directions. Say this out loud:

"It isn't broken. I was wrong."
