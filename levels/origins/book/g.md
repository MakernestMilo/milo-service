THE GIFT · 20 MIN
Three questions, in order

Write their answers in their words, not yours. That is the whole discipline of this session.

WHAT ANNOYS YOU?

Their first answer will be too big: "the house is too hot". Ask again. Ask where, and when, and what they do about it now.

WHEN DOES IT HAPPEN?

This is the question that turns a complaint into a sensor and a number. "In the afternoons" is a line waiting to be chosen.

HOW WOULD YOU KNOW IT WAS FIXED?

They answer this one, not you. Write it down exactly as they say it, even if it isn't what you hoped for.

THE GIFT · 15 MIN
The handover

Explain it once. Show them how to switch it on, what it does, and what the light or the noise means.

Then stop talking. No coaching, no hovering, no "you have to hold it like this". Let them use it while you watch, in silence, even when they do it wrong.

The silence is the hard part.

It is how you find out whether you built the right thing.

BREAK IT ON PURPOSE · 15 MIN
The brief was wrong

It almost always is. The line is off. It goes at the wrong moment. It solves the problem you heard, not the problem they have.

You get one change, made now, with them in the room, from what they say, not what you guess.

Ask them what is wrong with it, and change that one thing.

GIVEN

The machine goes, base and all. It belongs to them now. Leave them a phone charger and its cable, or show a grown-up in that house how the battery pack is charged. What you keep is this book, twelve cards, the chart, a photo, and the strip. The strip comes off the base and stays with you, because it is yours, not the machine's.
