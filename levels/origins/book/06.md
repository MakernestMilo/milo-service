The one idea

Some things don't have an amount. They have a number of times.

A thing that happens and is then over is called an event. Counting events sounds easy. It isn't, because somebody has to decide what counts as one, and the machine will never work that out for itself.

What makes one of something?

BREAK IT ON PURPOSE · 14 MIN
The double count

Two things to try. Both will go wrong.

First, open the door extremely slowly. One opening adds two or three. The magnet drifts along the edge of what the switch can feel, hovers there, and the switch changes its mind several times on the way past.

Second, move the magnet a little further away and swing the door normally. Now sometimes it counts nothing at all.

Nothing is broken. The machine has no idea what "one opening" means, because nobody has told it. You know. It doesn't.

So tell it. Move the magnet closer. Then find SETTLE on the dial. It starts at 0 seconds, which is why the wobble got through. Settle is how long the machine ignores the switch after each count. Too long, and two quick visits count as one. Too short, and the wobble comes back.

There is no perfect number. Only your number.

Show it

Tell your family how many times that door opened yesterday. Somebody will refuse to believe you. Somebody will ask whether the cat counts.

That argument is the point. "Does the cat count?" is exactly the same question as "what is one event?", and you are the person who gets to decide.
