The one idea

A machine can't feel warm or cold. It has no skin. It has no idea what warm means.

What it has is a sensor: a small part whose whole job is to turn something real into a number. The sensor feels the warmth. The number travels down a wire. The board reads the number.

04
10 MIN
First run, go hunting

Pick the whole machine up and carry it round the house. You are looking for one thing.

Where is the coldest place in this house?

Not a guess. Proof. Take it to at least four places and give the number a minute to settle each time. Near the fridge. The bathroom. A room with the fan on. A shelf up high. The floor by the door.

WHERE I TOOK IT
DEGREES

When you finish, you will know something about the house you live in that nobody else living in it knows, and you will be able to prove it.

--- page 16 ---
05
BREAK IT ON PURPOSE · 12 MIN
The number that isn't real

With the machine running, pull sensor A's blue wire off its pin. Just the blue one.

Look at the display. There is still a number. It looks completely normal. Nothing flashes. Nothing complains.

Now hold the sensor tightly in your fist to warm it. Watch for twenty seconds.

Nothing changes. The number froze the moment the wire came off, because the signal is what carries the number, and there is no signal any more. The display is showing you the last thing it heard.

A number on a screen is not proof that anything is being measured.

Put the blue wire back and watch it come alive. Now pull the display's brown wire instead. This time the screen goes dark.

Two things went wrong, and only one of them looked wrong. In chapter 11 somebody will break this machine without telling you how. You will be very glad you know this.

07
6 MIN
Show it

Find a grown-up and tell them where the coldest place in the house is. They will disagree. Show them the numbers.

Then trace the loop with your finger: sensor, blue wire, board, display. Tell them what each wire carries.

Pull the blue wire off and ask them if it is still working. They will say yes. Almost everybody does.

SAY THIS

"Pull this wire off. Is it still working? Look at the screen. It looks fine, doesn't it?"

4 MIN
Name it, and before you go on

One last thing. It is the reason there is a pen in the box.

This machine will be with you for fourteen chapters. It will end up with a body and a job. Give it a name now, while it can barely do anything. Write it on the name strip. The strip lives under your machine, between its legs.

BEFORE YOU GO ON

You should be able to point at the machine and say which wire carries what, without looking. If you can't yet, take the wires off and put them back. This is the one chapter where doing it twice is exactly right.

FOR THE GROWN-UP

The frozen-number failure is the most important twenty seconds in act one, and it only works if nobody warns the maker. If they ask whether they have broken it, say yes, and that the book will fix it in a minute. The naming matters too: chapter 11 depends on the maker caring about this machine.
