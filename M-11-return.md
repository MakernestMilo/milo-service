## And what it found about the harness

**The harness went quiet rather than red.** With the position defaulting to
step one, the prompt carried **86,016 characters against 100,633** across the
fourteen chapters, and the harness reported 7,616 · 0 fail either way.

**Every rule proves an absence, and less material is fewer places for something
to be present.** It would have reported success on any change that removed
material. `qc.py` now stands the child where the failure is, which is its own
subject, and is unchanged for the right reason.

`gate_publicity` had the same fault and **its own fixture caught it** —
chapter 10's pre-authored ask fell from a four-word run to *it off*. First time
in six orders that has gone in that direction.

**Then the harness went loudly red, and was right.** Serving `TEACH` tripped
3,264 checks on R2, and `TEACH['logging interval']` did state chapter 07's
withheld cause as a principle. The architect rewrote the entry rather than
accept the withholding, and it cleared all four cause words **without being
aimed at them** — because the sentence stopped stating the chapter's answer.

## Five items, closed

**1 · The position.** `Session` carries it, it comes from the card, it advances
only on what the child says, and `f.get("stage")` appears nowhere in
`assembler.py`. **BD was then amended for the library case**: the position
belongs to the machine on the table, not the session, and only the child can
say what it is. That amendment is M-12's.

**2 · The fixture that opens before the failure.** Fourteen authored openers,
none starting a clock in any chapter. **D's carries the trap** — *where do i
begin* is one word from *where do i start*, which is in `NEG` and fires in all
fourteen — and a test asserts both halves, so the trap is documented by
something that runs.

**3 · Carry-forward.** Two of three not observed. **The first was carried
wrongly by me across two orders**: `runtime.OVERRIDE` is the beta's list
character for character and reaches L3, or L4 where there is no fix. What is
real is a phrase list that hears *please just say* and not *please*.

**4 · R10's subject.** Resolved as above.

**5 · Teaching.** The ohm refusal is gone — **5 of 5 answer against 1 of 5**,
`TEACH` overlap 0.05 → 0.37–0.42 — with **no word of VOICE changed**, because
the permission had existed since M-01 and had never had a glossary. **0 of 10
put a resistor in a box that has none**, and every reply that mentions one says
so unprompted. **P1 and P3 both holding is the finding: the parts guard was
overreaching rather than protecting.**

**P2 failed and is the more interesting half.** *Pull the third and the sensor
still works perfectly, but nobody is listening to it* reached **0 of 5**, and
the overlap moved 0.38–0.56 to 0.38–0.62 — almost not at all. **Presence is not
use.** Where Milo does not know a thing, serving it changes everything; where
Milo knows it and the corpus says it better, serving it changes nothing.

**And BH is real in the assembler and did not reach the child.** The
eighteen-part claim never arrives. What arrives is the chapter's own stage:
chapter 11's *wake the machine* to a cold start gets **2 of 5** told they have
already done it and **0 of 5** asked whether they have, against **0** and **5
of 5** for chapter 01. **The difference is the stage, not `part_sets()`.**

## What this order cost me, and it is the honest part

written in M-10 step 01, repeated in the readiness note, carried into
`M-10-carried.md`, put into this order, and told to the architect twice. Seven
tests now assert it phrase by phrase.

**A prediction with a hole in it.** Step 05a's Q1 passed at ≥3 of 5 and was
falsified below 2, and the measurement was exactly 2.

**Two scans that read the wrong key and reported absence.** Both stated before

fired on it.

**A commit made while a test was failing**, because `pytest -q | tail -2`
returns `tail`'s exit code. Third instance of that shape in this project.

**And a ratio threshold chosen to sit above what had been measured**, rewritten
to assert the named cases.

Five, in an order whose subject was instruments that cannot see themselves.
